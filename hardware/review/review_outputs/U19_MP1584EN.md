## Findings

### F1 — Exposed thermal pad had no net · **HIGH** · confidence high · **FIXED**
`GetNetname()` on U19's pad "9" returned empty (netcode 0). MPS's PIN FUNCTIONS table
(p.4) lists **one** row — *"5 | GND / Exposed Pad | … Connect exposed pad to GND plane for
optimal thermal performance"* — so GND and the pad are the same node. Impact on a 3 A
switcher with θJA = 50 °C/W: the primary heat path is broken, the GND pour never stitches
to the pad, and the assembler has no netlist instruction to connect it. Neither DRC nor
ERC flags this, because the symbol has no 9th pin to be unconnected.

**Fix applied:** the EP was renumbered to pad **"5"** and set to net GND, and its zone
connection set to **solid** (not thermal-relief spokes — spokes would throttle the very
heat path we are fixing). Chosen over adding a pin to the symbol because it needs no
symbol edit, no netlist re-import, and it survives "update from schematic". DRC re-run:
**parity 0**, so schematic/PCB agreement is intact.
⚠️ **This lives in the board's embedded footprint copy.** Running *Update Footprints from
Library* on U19 would revert it. Re-run `review/tools/q.py main ep` after any such update.

### F2 — Four netless pads under U19 are solder-paste apertures · **not a defect**
The other four "unconnected" pads are on **F.Paste only**, no copper — the standard 4-window
paste pattern that stops the part floating on a solder bump during reflow. Correct as
drawn. (A net briefly assigned to them during the fix was reverted.)

### F3 — Compensation deviates from MPS Table 3 · **warning** · confidence high
Table 3's 5 V row: R3 = 100 kΩ, C3 = 150 pF, with L = 15–22 µH and C2 = 22 µF.
Board: **R3 = 51 kΩ, C3 = 3.3 nF** (L100 = 22 µH ✓, C108 = 22 µF ✓).
Working MPS's own formulas backwards (GEA = 60 µA/V, GCS = 9 A/V, VFB = 0.8 V):
- crossover fC ≈ **32 kHz** (MPS targets fS/10 = 90 kHz; their own R3 = 100 k gives ~62 kHz)
- compensation zero 1/(2π·R3·C3) ≈ **950 Hz**, where the procedure intends fC/4 ≈ 8 kHz

Net effect: a **slower, more heavily damped loop** than the reference. That errs safe — it
will not oscillate — but it was not derived from the datasheet procedure and transient
response will be poor. For a rail feeding a Pico and LEDs that hardly matters.
**Recommendation:** leave as-is for the first article, and do a **load-step test at
bring-up** (0.1 A → 1 A, scope the 5 V rail) before committing to volume.

### F4 — EN divider margin at the low end of the stated input range · **warning** · high
R101/R114 = 100 k/24.9 k is **exactly MPS's Figure 4 divider**, which MPS labels for an
**8 V–28 V** input. It is deliberate: it programs input UVLO to ≈ VOUT + 2.5 V, close to
the datasheet's "VIN−VOUT > 3 V" rule for keeping the bootstrap cap refreshed at light
load. **Do not "improve" it** — raising R114 would lower UVLO and break that.

The consequence is that the board's advertised **9–18 V** range is optimistic at the bottom:
- turn-on needs EN ≥ 1.65 V worst case → **VA ≥ 8.27 V**
- at VA = 18 V, EN = 3.59 V, safely under the 6 V abs max ✓

A 9 V adapter sagging under load, minus the P-FET drop, can approach 8.3 V. Combined with
the LM13700's 9.5 V single-supply minimum and the TL074 common-mode limit (see the main
report), **the honest input spec for this pedal is 12 V, not 9 V.**

### F5 — Input decoupling is exemplary · **pass**
C104 10 µF/50 V X7R 1206 plus C111 100 nF on VA is exactly what p.11–12 asks for,
including the 50 V rating for DC-bias derating at 18 V. Catch diode SS34 (40 V/3 A) matches
the recommended B340A class. Output 22 µF matches Table 3. BST 100 nF matches.

### F6 — Lifecycle · **manual_review**
The datasheet is watermarked *NOT RECOMMENDED FOR NEW DESIGNS — REFER TO MP2338* on every
page. Stock is fine today (LCSC C15051, ~6 k). MP2338 is SOT583/synchronous/450 kHz — a
different package and pinout, so this is a respin decision, not a substitution.
